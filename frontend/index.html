Project Overview & Academic Context

Project: AI-Based Phishing Website Detection • Authors: Daksh Jaiswal (RA2411003030048) & Unnatti Dixit (RA2411003030009) • Institution: SRM Institute of Science and Technology

Problem Statement

Phishing attacks deceive users into relinquishing sensitive credentials and financial tokens. Conventional defense techniques (static blocklists, manual heuristics, signature matching) suffer from acute latency gaps: empirical studies show over 63% of phishing campaigns conclude within 2 hours, while blacklists catch under 20% of fresh phish at hour zero.

Scope & Design Principles

  • Pure URL-Only Features: Extraction relies entirely on raw URL strings (lengths, punctuation, entropy, keywords, digit ratios). No DOM scraping, HTML parsing, or runtime WHOIS lookups.
  • Zero Network Dependency at Prediction: Prevents network latency overhead and eliminates privacy/evasion leaks to adversary servers.
  • Controlled Offline Evaluation: Evaluated on the benchmark dataset from Hannousse & Yahiouche (2021) with identical stratified splits.
  • Explainability First: Transparent risk scoring with granular feature explanations to enable user verification.

Limitations & Honest Security Boundaries

A "Legitimate" classification does NOT guarantee absolute safety: Attackers can compromise reputable domains or deploy compromised subdomains that mimic benign lexical patterns. Detection operates as an advisory layer, not a replacement for multi-factor authentication (MFA) and endpoint defense.

Future Scope

  1. Lightweight Page-Content Signals: Selectively ingest HTML metadata (e.g. login form targets, external favicon links) if latency budget allows.
  2. Deep Learning Sequences: Character-level and subword CNNs/Transformers (such as URLNet) trained on larger streaming feeds.
  3. Continuous Concept-Drift Retraining: Automated pipelines to digest daily PhishTank/OpenPhish feeds and update weights dynamically.
  4. Cross-Dataset Generalization: Training on one distribution (e.g. Hannousse 2021) and validating robustness on independent enterprise telemetry.
